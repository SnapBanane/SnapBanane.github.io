Twelve robots, one ball, a camera above.

In Division B, two teams of six robots play on a nine by six metre field. Overhead cameras track every robot and the ball, and from then on the software decides everything. Nobody holds a remote.

[1.2]

Read the game.

I write strategy for BohleBots in TypeScript. That means working out where the ball will be, how long each robot needs to reach a point, and who should go: interception, a ball approach that drives around obstacles, a defender that covers.

[1.3]

Find the pass.

Every teammate is a pass option, and most of them are bad ones. I'm prototyping a gradient-boosted model, trained on logs of real league matches, that predicts which passes will arrive.

Work in progress
[1.4]

Take the shot.

Before a robot kicks, the strategy splits the opponent goal into sectors and looks for the gaps the keeper and defenders leave open. Then it shoots at the widest one.
